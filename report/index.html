What registrants do on the S7 page

Every US user who reached S6 (qualifier form done) lands on the S7 page: pick a time slot, add a LinkedIn URL, upload a resume, finish. This report counts who did what there, and how each group converted to a sale and attended the webinar against all registrants. 13,387 registrants, 1 August – 28 September 2026.

5. Hypothesis and insights

Hypothesis tested: what a registrant does on the S7 page — picking a slot, adding LinkedIn, uploading a resume, submitting — is related to whether they attend the webinar (WA %) and whether they buy (sales conversion).

Verdict: supported, for both. The more of the S7 page a registrant completes, the more likely they are to attend and to buy. The link is stronger for sales (up to 2.1× all registrants) than for attendance (up to 1.24×). It holds in August and in September, in paid and in non-paid — 39 of 40 input-by-slice checks point the same way. It is a correlation: it identifies the stronger leads, it does not prove the page creates them.

01

A clear dose-response. Touching none of the three inputs converts at 1.37% with 41.9% attendance — below all registrants on both. Each extra input lifts both, up to 4.14% and 56.8% for all three. The 1,330 users who touched all three are 13% of S6 users but bring 23% of S6 sales; the 3,646 who touched none are 36% of S6 users and 21% of S6 sales.

02

The resume upload is the strongest single signal — the upload, not the click. Uploaders convert at 4.00% (2.0× all registrants) and attend at 56.3%. Users who opened the file picker but never uploaded convert at 2.46% and attend at 49.8% — lower on both (conversion p = 0.038, attendance p = 0.002). In paid, users who clicked but did not upload convert at 1.09% — below the paid average of 1.40% — while paid uploaders convert at 2.73%.

03

Skipping the slot marks the weakest S6 users. Slot selection is the most common input: 3.01% vs 1.37% conversion and 51.0% vs 42.7% attendance. S6 users who pick no slot convert below all registrants. LinkedIn behaves the same way (3.35% vs 1.85%).

04

Stopping after S6 is the sharpest attendance warning. Users who complete S6 but never submit S7 attend at 32.8% against 50.5% for those who submit (p < 0.001), and convert at about half the rate. Stopping at S4 is lower still for attendance (25.3%). Stopping between S5 and S6 hurts conversion (0.87% vs 2.30%) but barely moves attendance (45.1% vs 47.4%).

05

What this supports, and what it does not. S7-page behaviour is a cheap lead-quality signal, available minutes after registration: uploaders and all-three users are the best prospects for follow-up, while no-input and stopped-after-S6 users are the likeliest to miss the webinar. It does not show that making people fill these inputs would raise sales — motivated users both fill more and buy more. That needs an A/B test, for example a stronger slot or resume prompt for half of traffic.

Limits. Non-paid is small (430 registrants, 25 sales), so its rows are directional. September leads have had less time to close and attend. Some people appear on two devices; counted per person, conversion rates move by 0.1pt or less.
